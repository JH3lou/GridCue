These are the terms for what a User asks and how GridCue reads it. The examples use GridCue's synthetic wealth grid, with columns such as Registration type, Custodian, Advisor, Market value and Unrealized gain.

## Utterance

The raw text of one request, as the **User** (the person at the keyboard) typed or dictated it.

> "Taxable accounts over $1 million, grouped by advisor"

GridCue keeps the Utterance for the current interaction. It is not logged or put in audit events unless the **Host**, your application, opts in. Dictation tools write into the same text field, so a dictated request is just an Utterance with channel `"dictated"`.

## Clause

One part of an Utterance that asks for one kind of change. Code splits Clauses deterministically, at punctuation, "then", and "and" or a comma before a verb such as "sort", "group" or "hide". It also splits mid-line, with no punctuation, where a different kind of change starts after an instruction ("sorted", "grouped", "show", "only", "filter", "where"). The User sees a Clause called a "part".

| Utterance | Clauses |
| --- | --- |
| "Taxable accounts over $1 million, grouped by advisor" | "taxable accounts over $1 million" · "grouped by advisor" |
| "Group by custodian, then advisor" | "group by custodian" · "group by advisor" (the second part borrows the first one's verb) |
| "Grouped by household sorted largest to smallest" | "grouped by household" · "sorted largest to smallest" |
| "Roth IRAs grouped by rep" | one Clause: a value before another change's verb describes which rows |
| "Show accounts where value is over $1M" | one Clause: "show" and "where" both choose rows |
| `Advisor containing "Smith. Jones"` | one Clause: quoted text is never split and never read as an instruction |

Comparisons are read as words or symbols, spaced or not: "over", "under", "at least", "at most", "at or above", "no greater than", "greater than or equal to", "since", `>`, `<`, `>=`, `<=`, `=`, and "$1M or more" or "$1M max" after the amount. A minus sign or "negative" right before an amount makes it negative ("gain below -$50k"); a hyphen between two amounts is a range ("$50k-$100k", "10-20%"). An amount is read only when what follows it is the end of the part or something GridCue reads, such as a name or "grouped by"; "$1M, roughly" and "over $1M or so" are asked about. With no comparison it is "equal to" only when it sits right by its column ("market value $750k", "opened on 2024-01-01"). Otherwise, as with "around $1M", `!= $1M` or "$1M give or take", GridCue asks. Filters always combine with "and", so "over $1M or at Northgate" is asked about too. "Hide taxable accounts" and "exclude accounts over $1M" keep the other rows when they leave out one simple condition; anything more is asked about.

Quoted text filters a column for rows containing it, and only when the column's name comes right before it. Nothing, ":", "is", "are", or "containing", "contains", "includes", "has", "like" or "matching" may sit between them: `advisor "Lane"`, `advisor: "Lane"` and `advisor containing "Lane"` all read as containing. "Column" after a column's name, and "name" after a column that names another record ("advisor name"), are part of the name. Any other word between them is asked about, because GridCue only matches by containing: `advisor starts with "Av"`, `advisor is exactly "Avery Lane"` and `advisor = "Avery Lane"` ask. The grid's own `rowNoun` right before "for" and quoted text means the rows (`accounts for "Avery Lane"`), and quoted text with no column named before it asks which column should contain it. A provider's pick for that column is offered first, never applied. The words around text with no column are checked first, as below, and a containing word may also sit right before it (`containing "Lane"`): `starts with "Av"`, `exactly "Avery Lane"` and `"Avery Lane" exactly` ask about those words instead. The words right before the column and right after the text must be read too. Before the column: a verb that chooses rows ("show", "keep", "include", "view", "look at"), "only", "all of", the grid's own `rowNoun`, a word that joins the rows to the column, "for", "and" or "but only", and a direction or count (`taxable accounts with advisor "Lane"`, `value over $1M for advisor "Lane"`). The joining words are "with", "where", "whose", "having", "holding", "that have", "which have", and "that", "which" or "who" before "are" or "is". "Filter" also takes "rows", "records", "the grid" or "the table", then "by", "on", "for", "to", "in", "with" or "where" (`filter by advisor "Lane"`). After the text, up to the next name or amount: "'s", the `rowNoun`, "and", "or", "then" or "also", "but" before "only" or a sort, a joining word, a sort or grouping ("sorted by", "in order of"), "show", "hide" or "unhide" before a name, a direction or count ("largest first", "top 10"), a preposition before a named value ("at Northgate", "in the taxable accounts"), the words that leave a named value out ("but trusts"), "not", "no", "non" or "without" before a yes/no name, after "but" too, and a comparison before an amount, after "valued" or "worth" too (`advisor "Lane" sorted by value`, `advisor "Lane" at Northgate`). At the end of the part: any of "please", "too", "also", "only" and "as well", then "thanks". Anything else is asked about: `accounts sans advisor "Lane"`, `the opposite of advisor "Lane"`, `advisor "Lane" exactly`, `advisor "Lane" accounts exactly` and `advisor "Lane" but restricted` ask. So do some correct requests, such as `advisor's name is "Lane"`, `advisor containing the text "Lane"`, `filter advisor to "Lane"` and `show accounts for advisor "Lane"`; write `advisor containing "Lane"` instead.

A negation in a part with quoted text asks, such as `advisor is not "Avery Lane"`, "isn't", `!=`, "other than", "except", "without" and "excluded", with two exceptions. A negation the grammar reads as leaving a named value out is read: "non-retirement", `advisor "Lane" not at Northgate`, `advisor "Lane" but trusts`, `advisor "Lane" not including trusts`. So is "not", "no", "non" or "without" before a yes/no name (`advisor "Lane" not restricted`). `advisor "Lane" except restricted` and `advisor "Lane" except for trusts` ask.

A "!" that starts a request, follows a space, or sits right before a name, quoted text or an amount says "not", and GridCue asks about it, with quoted text or without: `!trusts`, `!advisor "Lane"` and `advisor "Lane" !trusts` ask. A "!" right after a word ends the sentence ("show trusts!"), and `!=` is a comparison.

A date is a day. A date compared with a date-and-time column ("last contact after 2026-01-31" on a column that holds times of day) is asked about, since the time of day would decide which rows stay. A date that is not a real day ("2026-02-31") is asked about too.

Size words set a sort direction: "largest to smallest", "biggest first", "top" and "largest" sort descending; "smallest to largest", "smallest first" and "smallest" ascending. With no column named, they sort by the schema's [`defaultMeasure`](/docs/api/schema#schemaoptions), or GridCue asks which column. A count after a size word ("top 10", "largest 5") is a count, never a filter value: it becomes [`limit.set`](/docs/api/protocol#viewoperation) where the adapter supports it.

A count stays on the view until something clears it, so a change to the rows it keeps asks first. After "top 10 accounts by market value", a request that changes or clears the sort, or adds, replaces or clears a filter ("sort by advisor", "clear the sort", "only the taxable ones", "clear the filters") could mean the same ten rows in a new order, or the first ten of the new view. GridCue can't tell, so it asks "Keep only the first 10 rows of the new view, or show every row?" with two choices, "Keep the first 10" and "Show every row". Each choice adds its own line to the Preview. Grouping, hiding or showing columns and "reset the view" don't change which rows the count keeps, so they don't ask, and a request that sets its own count ("top 5 by gain") doesn't either. The rule holds for every provider: it is applied to the plan, after the provider has answered.

Two forms set the count directly, and code reads both without a provider. "Show every row", "show all rows", "remove the count" and "no count" clear it, with the line "Show every row". "Show only the first 5 rows" and "only the first 5 rows" set it, with the line "Show only the first 5 rows"; with no sort on the view, GridCue asks "First 5 by which column?" instead, and on a grid that can't keep a count it says "This grid can't show only the first 5 rows." and changes nothing. The forms are read in the order they are said. "Sort by advisor, then show only the first 5 rows" shows the first 5 by Advisor. "Show only the first 5 rows, then sort by advisor" could mean the 5 rows on screen now, or the first 5 by Advisor, so GridCue asks about that part, as it does about count words said before "reset the view", which would undo them. Where there is no count to clear, "show every row" on a view with filters may mean the rows the filters leave out, so it goes to the provider, as do the clearing words on a grid that can't keep a count. A count form with a word left over, "remove the limit" for one, goes to the provider and usually asks. "Show all accounts", with the grid's `rowNoun`, is asked about too: on a view with a filter and a count it asks "Show every row, or clear the filters too?".

The kind of change a Clause asks for (filter, sort, group, show, hide, show only, clear, or reset) is its **Operation Family**. A provider scores each family; the compiler decides which ones are used.

A request can have up to 12 Clauses.

## Mention

A column or enum value that a Clause names by one of the Host's declared labels or aliases. Code finds Mentions before any provider call, matching whole words and plurals.

In "Roth accounts at Northgate, sorted by rep":

- "Roth" is a Mention of Registration type = Roth IRA (an alias of the value);
- "Northgate" is a Mention of Custodian = Northgate;
- "rep" is a Mention of the Advisor column (an alias);
- "accounts" is **not** a Mention of Account number. With `rowNoun: "account"` declared, it means the rows.

A name used for the rows themselves is never a Mention. The rules lean toward "the rows" on purpose: a name wrongly read as the rows falls back to the provider, while a name wrongly read as a column would propose a wrong view.

## Clarification

A single focused question GridCue asks when it can't resolve a request without a choice only the User can make. It often offers bounded options.

| Request | Clarification |
| --- | --- |
| "Show the big accounts" | "What should “show the big accounts” filter on? Include a value, such as an amount or a category." |
| "Largest households first" | "Did you mean households as a whole? GridCue can group by Household." |
| "Accounts over $1 million", when the provider can't tell which column the amount applies to | "Which column should be above $1,000,000?" |
| "Market value around $1M" | "I'm not sure how to compare with “$1m”. Try “over”, “under”, “at least”, or “exactly”." |
| `Advisor starts with "Av"` | "I'm not sure how to match “av”. Try “containing”." |
| `Advisor "Lane" exactly` | "I'm not sure what to do with “exactly”. Try rephrasing that part." |
| `Advisor is not "Avery Lane"` | "GridCue can't filter for text that isn't there. Ask for the text to look for instead." |
| `Containing "Lane"`, with no column named | "Which column should contain “lane”?" |
| `!trusts` | "GridCue can't tell what “!” leaves out. Say which rows to show." |
| "Opened after 2026-02-31" | "2026-02-31 isn't a date." |
| "Last contact after 2026-01-31", on a date-and-time column | "Last contact holds times of day, so GridCue can't filter it by date yet." |
| "Accounts over $2M or at Northgate" | "GridCue can only show rows that match every condition, not either one. Ask for one at a time." |
| "Group by custodian over $1M", when nothing in the plan uses the amount | "I'm not sure what to do with “$1m”. Try rephrasing that part." |

GridCue does not invent a threshold for "big" or pick a column for "$1 million" on a guess. Before a plan is ready, GridCue also checks that its operations use every amount, quoted text, date, count and name it read from the request, and asks about anything left over by its words. The check catches a dropped part, not a part read the wrong way, so the comparison and column rules above still matter. Answering is one call:

```ts
cue.answer(clarificationId, optionId);
```

A plan with an open Clarification has status `needs_clarification` and no operations, so it can't be applied.

## Abstention

GridCue declining to propose any change, because the request is too ambiguous or too low-confidence to act on safely. Decisions below the `clarify` confidence band are discarded, not applied.

> "What's the weather?" → "I'm not sure what to change for “what's the weather”. Try asking to filter, sort, group, or show or hide columns."

If the User answers "No" to a Clarification and nothing is left, GridCue says "Okay, nothing will change," and keeps the request for editing. An Abstention is never reported as a success.

## A grid that can't group

A Grid Adapter lists the operations it supports in `getCapabilities().operations`. When `group.set` isn't in that list, the grid can't group rows. A part of a request that asks to group or ungroup then ends in one Clarification with no choices, since no answer makes the grid able to:

| Request, on a grid without `group.set` | Clarification |
| --- | --- |
| "Group by advisor" | "This grid can't group rows." |
| "Ungroup" | "This grid can't group rows." |
| "Group by advisor and sort by market value" | "This grid can't group rows." |
| "Accounts over $1M grouped by custodian" | "This grid can't group rows." |
| "Sort by market value, groups by advisor" | "This grid can't group rows." |
| "Show the Northgate group" | "This grid can't group rows." |

Nothing in the request applies, the other parts included. The User asked for every change, and showing only some would be a different view. Ask again without the grouping to get the rest.

Any word built on "group" counts, as a verb or a noun: "grouping", "groups", "regroup", "groupwise". "The Northgate group" and "groups of taxable accounts" may mean a grouping or only those rows, and GridCue can't tell which, so it asks. Ask for "Northgate accounts" to get the filter. "By" right after the word always asks: "keep the sort and the grouping by advisor".

Some of these words GridCue can't read at all: "regroup" or "groupwise" with no "by" next, the text after "contains", "named" or "like", or a grouping said to stay with more after it. Such a request keeps the question it would ask anyway: "regroup my thoughts" and "households containing group" ask what to change. It never ends in a ready Preview, though. Where the rest of the request would be ready, it asks "This grid can't group rows." instead: "sort by gain, regroup on advisor", "sort by gain, by advisor groupwise", "sort by gain, then advisor like groups", "sort by gain, the grouping stays on advisor".

These don't count, and the request reads as on any grid:

- A grouping said to stay or not wanted, with nothing after it in its part but punctuation or a joining word such as "and": "clear the sort, the grouping stays", "clear the filters but keep groups", "sort by gain without grouping". Anything more after it asks, even another thing to keep: "clear the sort, leave the grouping and the filter alone".

  Only these words count. Right before the grouping word, with only "the", "my" and the like between: "keep", "leave", "don't touch", "don't change", "no change to", "retain", "preserve", "maintain", "without changing", "without touching", "not", "no", "without", "w/o", "except", "excluding", "apart from", "other than", and "all", "everything" or "anything but". Or right after it: "stays", "remains", "as is", "alone", "unchanged", "untouched", "intact". Other wording asks, such as "ignore the grouping" or "forget the grouping", which may mean removing it. Leaving out an ungrouping asks too: "sort by gain, not ungrouped" asks for groups.
- "Group" inside a name the Host declared, such as a value "Group Annuity" or a column "Group name", or inside quoted text: `households containing "group"` filters.
- A column's own label beside one of its values, where a column is called "Group": "the alpha group", "group = beta".

A request with no word built on "group" ("filter taxable accounts", "sort by gain") is read as on any other grid.

The hint in "I'm not sure what to change" names only what the grid offers. On a grid with `group.set`, "What's the weather?" gets "Try asking to filter, sort, group, or show or hide columns." On a grid without it, the same request gets "Try asking to filter, sort, or show or hide columns." The hint after a request that changes nothing ("Try filtering, sorting, grouping, or showing or hiding columns.") is cut the same way. A grid that lists none of those operations gets no "Try" sentence.

## Unsupported Segment

A part of an Utterance that asks for something outside the view-only boundary. GridCue recognises four kinds: editing data, workflow actions (trading, emailing, approving), navigation, and export. A request that names a restricted column is refused the same way.

> "Show restricted holdings and place the trades"

GridCue understands the first part, but "place the trades" is a workflow action. The **whole** plan is refused, with "GridCue only changes how the table looks, so it can't do “place the trades”. Remove that part to continue." GridCue never applies the allowed part quietly and implies the request succeeded.
